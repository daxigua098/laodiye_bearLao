## 1. The idea
A calm page where the pictures are placed like prints on a gallery wall: each at its own size and position, with text set small and light between them. Nothing lines up in the obvious way, yet everything sits on a grid you can feel. The unevenness is what makes it read as art-directed by a person: a small portrait far left, a wide landscape running off the right edge, a square that overlaps the next section. It lets photographs lead without ever becoming a slideshow.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
A 12-column grid with wide margins (5–7vw). The hero is a single line of large light type (6–9vw) across the top third, and below it one photograph placed off-centre (columns 6–12, 16:10), its top edge overlapping the baseline zone of the headline by a few pixels of deliberate tension. Each following section places two or three photographs with different spans and vertical offsets: for example a 3:4 portrait in columns 2–5 starting 120px lower than a 16:10 landscape in columns 7–12; then a single small 1:1 in columns 9–11 beside a paragraph in columns 2–6. Paragraphs are short (max 40ch) and sit in the gaps. No two consecutive sections use the same arrangement. Vertical spacing is large and irregular on purpose (120–260px).

## 3. Type — Zodiak / Hanken Grotesk
Hairline serif capitals set large enough that the thin strokes read as drawing, with a small neutral sans for everything practical. Suits lodges, spas, architecture and interiors; keep it on flat pale or dark fields where the strokes have full contrast.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.googleapis.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.gstatic.com" crossorigin>
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://api.fontshare.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://cdn.fontshare.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Hanken+Grotesk:wght@400;500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=zodiak@100,300&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Zodiak** — weight 100 above 96px, 300 for 28–36px statements · tracking -0.01em · leading 1.0 · capitals for the headline, sentence case for statements; never bold
- Text: **Hanken Grotesk** — 400 for body, 500 for emphasis · leading 1.5 · tracking 0
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.125rem, 1.05rem + 0.3vw, 1.3125rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
A light or regular weight at large size for the few headings; small, quiet text. Serif or a slender sans. Everything left-aligned to grid lines.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.

## 6. Hero — Small name, open sky
A single photograph fills the viewport edge to edge with no frame: choose one whose upper 45% is sky, wall or water with nothing in it and whose subject sits low and off-centre. All type is white and stays out of that empty zone. Top-left at a 24px margin, the name is stacked on two or three lines at 14px, like a letterhead. Top-right, three or four text links run in one line at 14px separated by commas, the current one marked by a 2px underline. Bottom-left, 32px from the left and bottom edges, the name repeats as the only larger type: two tight lines at clamp(44px, 4.2vw, 64px), line-height 1.0, regular weight, on columns 1–5. On the same baseline at the bottom-right margin sits the single action as an underlined text link with an arrow, and above it one supporting sentence at 15px, right-aligned, max 32ch. If a lower corner is too bright for white type, darken that corner alone with a neutral scrim. The page below opens on plain paper with one narrow paragraph and wide empty margins.

On a phone: The photograph is cropped to portrait around its subject and fills 100svh, its upper half still empty. The small stacked name stays top-left at 13px and the links collapse to one word top-right. The larger name sits bottom-left at 9.5vw on two lines, 20px in. The supporting sentence and the underlined action stack beneath it at 15px, left-aligned, 24px above the bottom edge.
Headline: The name set small on two lines of up to about 16 characters each at 4.2vw; the photograph does the work of a headline.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names colours (white): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).
- It draws lines or borders: keep them only inside a table, a form or a `data-ui` fragment; elsewhere separate with space or a change of ground.
- Its action is a text link: the primary action on the first screen is a real button (filled or solid-bordered, 44px or taller), placed where the blueprint puts the link. A second action may stay a link.

### 1. features — One-size name columns (index)
Pure text at one size. Three columns start on columns 1, 4 and 7 of the 12-column grid, leaving columns 10–12 empty on purpose. Each column opens with a group name in full ink and continues with a tight stack of six to ten capability names in the soft ink, all at 16–17px, regular weight, line-height 1.25, no bullets and no symbols; one blank line separates group name from stack. Hovering a name brings it to full ink and shows a one-line description in a fixed spot: the top of the empty columns 10–12, at the same size. The heading is no larger than 28px; two or three words, alone on column 1, 96px above the columns. Give the section 20vh of empty ground above and below, so the smallness reads as decided. Works on light or deep grounds.
On a phone: Columns stack with 40px between groups; text rises to 18px with line-height 1.4 for touch. Group names stay in full ink. Hover descriptions are dropped; each group ends instead with one sentence in the soft ink that sums it up. The heading is 24px, 48px above.

### 2. showcase — Frame run, one lifted (strip)
Columns 1–3 are a nearly empty column: the heading (two to four words at 24–28px) at the top, a link under it, and one or two short notes at 15px much further down, each level with the frame it describes. Columns 4–12 hold a run of six to eight frames of the same subject at one fixed height of 30vh, with natural widths and 6px gaps: the whole view, a tight crop of the same thing, a detail, the reverse side. The run is allowed to leave the right edge. One frame is lifted out of the row: shown at 2.2 times the row height with its bottom aligned to the row's bottom (flex row, align-items: end), so it stands above the others. Under each frame, at 15px: a name on the left and a single fact on the right. Square corners, no captions on the images.
On a phone: The heading and link sit on top. The lifted frame comes first at full width. The rest of the run scrolls sideways beneath it at a fixed height of 34vw with 4px gaps and scroll-snap, bleeding off the right edge. Notes are listed under the run at 15px.

### 3. about — Wide photo, colour tile, facts (mosaic)
Top: a heading of two to four words at clamp(40px, 4vw, 60px) on the left margin, then 24px below it a three-line lead paragraph at 21–22px across columns 1–6. 72px lower, two tiles sit side by side with a 16px gap. The left tile spans columns 1–8: a landscape photograph at 16:10 with one radius (0 or 10px). A short paragraph of up to 35 words at 16px is set in a solid paper-colour block 320px wide with 20px padding, placed over the photograph's top-right corner and flush with both edges. The right tile spans columns 9–12 and is a flat palette colour, 60% of the photograph's height: it holds a simple inline SVG outline of the region or the floor plan as one filled shape, with one solid dot marking the location. Under the colour tile, four label and value pairs at 14px in a two-by-two grid: altitude, distance, season, local time.
On a phone: Heading at 9vw, lead at 18px. The photograph goes full width at 4:3 and its overlaid paragraph moves beneath it as ordinary text. The colour tile follows at full width and 3:2, and the four label and value pairs sit under it in two columns.

### 4. gallery — Staggered pairs in empty ground (stack)
No heading. Photographs are placed one or two per screen so that more than half of every screen stays empty ground. Screen one: a 3:4 portrait in columns 1–4 against the left margin, and a near-square 5:4 image in columns 7–12 against the right margin whose top edge starts 30vh lower than the portrait's. Screen two: a 3:2 landscape in columns 6–11 and, 24vh lower, a small 4:5 image in columns 2–4. Repeat the two arrangements, mirrored on the second pass, for six to ten photographs. Under each image's left corner, a one-line caption at 13px in the soft ink, 10px below the image. Vertical gaps between pairs are 22–28vh. Every edge sits on a column line and offsets come from only two values. Square corners. If motion is used, images enter with a short `clip-path` wipe from the bottom edge.
On a phone: A single column in which widths and sides alternate: 70% against the left margin, 84% against the right, 56% left, then 100% full-bleed, with 64px between images. Captions sit under each image's left corner at 13px. Portrait crops are preferred so each image fills most of the screen height.

### 5. faq — Wide heading, indented questions (list)
A heading of three to five words at about 5.5vw runs from the left margin on one or two lines and is the only large thing in the section. 80px beneath it the questions begin, indented to start at column 5 and running to column 12, so the left third under the heading stays empty. Each question is a row at 22–24px in the regular weight with a chevron of two 2px strokes at the far right; rows are separated by 36px of space and no rules. Opening a row lays a tile of the one-step tone behind that row and its answer (24px padding, extending 24px beyond the text on both sides) and shows the answer at 17px with a 48ch measure. Six questions. Nothing else shares the screen; the indent against the wide heading is the composition. Build with `<details>`.
On a phone: The heading sits at 10vw on two lines. Questions lose the indent and run full width at 19px with 28px between rows; the open row keeps its tone tile, now bleeding to both screen edges, with the answer at 16px inside it.

### 6. cta — Last photograph, text beside (full-bleed)
A final full-bleed photograph, 100vw by 90svh, chosen so its subject sits in the left half and its right half is calm. All text is confined to the right half and never crosses the centre line: in columns 8–12, anchored to the lower third, a closing line of three to five words at about 3.4vw in a light weight, a paragraph of two or three lines at 17px, and one filled button in the paper colour with ink text, about 52px tall. If the image behind the text is not calm enough, darken the whole photograph slightly with `filter: brightness(0.8)` rather than laying a panel behind the words. The section butts directly against a footer in one solid colour, so the photograph's bottom edge is a hard line. No logo, no second action.
On a phone: The photograph is 100vw by 70svh, cropped to keep its subject. The text leaves the image and sits beneath it on the footer's solid colour: closing line at 28px, paragraph at 17px, full-width button. Image and colour meet at a hard edge.

### 7. footer — Columns above, wordmark below (stack)
The footer inverts ground and ink and fills a whole viewport (min-height 100svh), arranged as three rows in a grid (auto 1fr auto). Top row, 40px from the edge: four small columns on the 12-column grid (address, navigation, social, hours or contact), each a plain list at 15–16px with its first line in the full ink and the rest in the softer ink. Middle row: empty. Bottom row: the wordmark alone, sized to run from the left margin to the right margin on one line. Set it as inline SVG text with a `viewBox` fitted to the word and `width: 100%`, or tune `font-size` in `vw` to the letter count, so it never wraps; its baseline sits 20px above a single line of legal text at 13px. With `position: sticky; bottom: 0` and a lower `z-index` than the page, the last section slides away to uncover it.
On a phone: Columns become two by two at 15px with 32px between rows, at the top. A gap of about 20svh follows. The wordmark spans the full width at the bottom; a name longer than ten letters stacks on two lines, each fitted to the width. Legal line beneath at 12px.

## 8. Motion — Slow crossfade
The principal photograph quietly changes: three or four images of the same framing crossfade over about 1.6 seconds, holding five to six seconds each, or the fixed image changes when its matching section arrives. It should feel like light shifting in a room. Pause when the tab is hidden; under reduced motion show only the first image.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Placement. The differing sizes and offsets create movement on a still page.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Uneven is not random: every image edge lands on a column line, and offsets come from a small set of values. On a phone, keep the unevenness with alternating widths (100%, 72% left, 60% right) rather than stacking everything full width.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
